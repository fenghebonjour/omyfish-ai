# Quebec Sport Fishing Regulations — Overview

This is a plain-language summary to help anglers understand how Quebec fishing
rules work. It is not the legal text of the regulation — always verify the
current catch limit, season, and length rule for your specific zone at
[quebec.ca](https://www.quebec.ca/en/tourism-and-recreation/sporting-and-outdoor-activities/sport-fishing)
or the app's own Regs lookup (which reads live data from the same source
government systems) before keeping a fish.

## Fishing zones

Quebec is divided into numbered fishing zones (Zone 1 through Zone 29, with a
few zones split into north/south or east/west halves). Catch limits, seasons,
minimum/maximum lengths, and allowed fishing devices are all set **per zone,
per species** — the same species can have a different daily limit in Zone 27
than in Zone 10. There is no single province-wide limit table; you always need
to know which zone you're fishing in.

## What a "limit" usually includes

A typical zone regulation entry has up to four parts:

- **Catch/possession limit** — how many of a species (or species group) you
  may keep per day, e.g. "6 in all" for walleye and sauger combined.
- **Length limit** — a minimum size, a maximum size, or a protected "slot"
  range you must release fish within, e.g. "37 cm to 53 cm inclusively."
- **Fishing device** — some species/zones restrict to angling only (rod and
  line), excluding methods like ice-fishing tip-ups or nets where those would
  otherwise be legal for other species.
- **Season/period** — the open dates for that species in that zone. Fishing a
  species outside its open period is prohibited even if you're otherwise
  licensed. A handful of species (e.g. Atlantic salmon, copper redhorse, and a
  few others in some zones) are prohibited from targeted fishing year-round.

## Waterbody-specific exceptions

Beyond the general zone rules, individual lakes and rivers can have their own
tighter (or looser) exceptions — e.g. a specific lake with a lower daily limit
to protect a stressed population, or a stretch of river closed to protect a
spawning run. These exceptions are looked up by waterbody name, separately
from the zone-wide table.

## Consumption advisories are a different question

A fish being legal to catch and keep does **not** mean it's advisable to eat
in unlimited quantity. Quebec's freshwater fish consumption guide tracks
mercury and other contaminant levels by species, fish size, and specific
waterbody, and recommends a maximum number of meals per month accordingly —
larger fish of the same species generally carry more contaminant load, so a
"safe" number for a small fish does not carry over to a larger one of the same
species from the same water. This is a health guideline, separate from the
legal catch/possession limit, and the two should not be confused.

## Licensing

A valid Quebec fishing licence (sport-fishing permit) is required for anyone
12 and older to fish in Quebec, with some exceptions (e.g. a few designated
free-fishing days per year). Non-residents pay a different fee than Quebec
residents. This app does not sell or verify licences — see quebec.ca for
current pricing and where to buy one.

## Where the numbers in this app come from

Catch limits and zone lookups are pulled live from the official MFFP
(Ministère des Forêts, de la Faune et des Parcs) systems at
peche.faune.gouv.qc.ca. Consumption advisory numbers come from the Données
Québec "Guide de consommation du poisson de pêche sportive en eau douce"
dataset. Both update on their own schedule (regulations typically republish
around April 1 each year) — treat this app's numbers as a convenience lookup
of the same official sources, not a replacement for checking directly if
something looks off or out of date.
